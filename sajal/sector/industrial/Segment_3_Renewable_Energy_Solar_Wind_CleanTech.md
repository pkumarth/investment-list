### Institutional Synthesis: Capital Allocation & Portfolio Positioning in Sector 3

1.  **The Solar Value-Chain Bottleneck (Why June 2026 Changes Everything):**
    *   India currently has **>75 GW of Solar Module Assembly** capacity, but only **~13–15 GW of operational Solar Cell** capacity. Under MNRE's **ALMM List-II mandate effective June 1, 2026**, all government, PSU, rooftop (PM Surya Ghar), and open-access projects *must* use Indian-made Solar Cells.
    *   **Winners:** Integrated cell-to-module leaders (**Premier Energies** with 3.2 GW scaling to 8 GW cells, **Waaree Energies** with 5.4 GW cells live, **Emmvee**, and **Websol** as a tactical merchant cell play) enjoy a protected 18–24 month profit window.
    *   **Losers / Trap:** Pure module assemblers without operational cell lines will get squeezed between rising domestic cell input costs and module oversupply.
    *   **Component Pick-and-Shovel Winner:** **Borosil Renewables**—every module maker (whether Waaree, Premier, Reliance, or Adani) needs 2mm tempered glass, and the **Dec 2024 Anti-Dumping Duty ($673/MT floor)** restores Borosil's domestic pricing power just as Bifacial modules double glass consumption per MW.
2.  **Why Wind is Structurally Superior to Solar on Supply-Chain Competition (2025–2030):**
    *   While 60+ companies are building solar module factories, **Onshore Wind Turbine Manufacturing in India is a tight oligopoly** (**Suzlon Energy** & **Inox Wind** domestically, alongside Envision/Senvion).     *   **Suzlon Energy** (Net Cash ₹1,100+ Cr, 5.5 GW order book, 40%-margin 15 GW OMS annuity) and **Synergy Green Industries** (casting 3–5 MW hubs/frames for Vestas, Siemens Gamesa, GE & Suzlon) are the cleanest ways to play the Wind super-cycle without balance-sheet leverage.
