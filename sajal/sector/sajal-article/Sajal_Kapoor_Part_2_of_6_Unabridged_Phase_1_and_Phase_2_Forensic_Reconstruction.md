### I. The Philosophical Architecture: Why Ordinary Blades Break

Lord Wenhui marvels at his skill, and Cook Ding replies:
> *"What your servant cares about is the Way (Dao), which goes beyond mere skill. When I first began carving oxen, all I could see was the whole ox. After three years, I no longer saw the whole ox. And now, I meet it with my spirit and don’t look with my eyes... A good cook changes his knife once a year—because he cuts. A mediocre cook changes his knife once a month—because he hacks. I have used this knife of mine for nineteen years and have carved several thousand oxen with it, and yet the blade is as sharp as if it had just come from the grindstone. At the joints there are spaces (interstices), and the blade of the knife has no thickness. If you insert what has no thickness into such spaces, then there is plenty of room—more than enough for the blade to play about in."*
