## 3. Behavioral & Cognitive Psychology Deep-Dive (Phases 5 & 6)

1. **Nassim Nicholas Taleb (*The Lucretius Problem* — Cognitive Anchoring on Past Maxima/Minima):**
   * Why did almost every institutional fund manager sell **Neuland Labs** way too early at ₹2,500 or **Garware Hi-Tech Films** at ₹800?
   * **Cognitive Mechanism (Episodic Memory Anchoring):** The human hippocampus stores historical reference ranges (e.g., *"Garware is a 10% OPM polyester film company; Neuland is a 15% OPM API company"*). When a business undergoes a **structural phase transition** (Garware shifting to consumer branded self-healing PPF; Neuland shifting to patented NCE CMS), the brain treats the old historical ceiling as a law of physics (**The Lucretius Problem**). Only by auditing the *new unit economics from first principles* can an investor break free of historical anchoring.
2. **Thomas Bayes & Annie Duke (*Thinking in Bets*, 2018 — "Conviction Is Not Binary"):**
   * Former professional poker champion and cognitive psychologist **Annie Duke** explains that amateurs view beliefs as **100% True or 100% False**, whereas professionals view every position as a **calibrated probability distribution (e.g., 68% confidence, updated quarterly)**.
   * **Portfolio Application:** Sajal Kapoor’s **3-Stage Bayesian Position-Sizing Ladder** (1–2% Scout $\rightarrow$ 5–8% Proof-Gate Core $\rightarrow$ 10–18% Extremistan Winner, trimmed back at Johari peak) eliminates the emotional whipsaw of retail "all-in / all-out" gambling.
3. **Kurt Lewin (*Field Theory in Social Science* — Why "Numbers Are the Last Domino to Fall" in *Jubilant Ingrevia*):**

Waiting for trailing GAAP accounting numbers (Domino 5) to confirm a turnaround means you are buying *after* all the internal risk has already been resolved and priced in by strategists who tracked Dominoes 1 through 4.
