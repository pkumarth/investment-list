### MODULE 2: The EV Obsolescence vs. Lightweighting Audit (Ferrous Engine Risk vs. Aluminum Boom)

An internal combustion engine (ICE) and multi-speed manual gearbox contain **~180–250 kg of forged steel and cast iron** (cylinder block, cylinder head, crankshaft, camshaft, connecting rods, piston pins, valve-train). In a Battery Electric Vehicle (BEV), **100% of these parts vanish**. However, to compensate for a 350–500 kg lithium-ion battery pack, **total aluminum content per car increases by 35%–50% (from ~140 kg in ICE to ~210–250 kg in EVs)** across complex, thin-walled structural castings (e-motor housings, inverter enclosures, battery trays, shock towers).

#### 2. The Aluminum Die-Casting Turnaround Battle: Why Craftsman Wins While Sundaram-Clayton & Rico Bleed
Why does **Craftsman Automation** generate **16%–19% consolidated EBITDA margins and ~18%–22% ROCE**, whereas **Sundaram-Clayton** suffers negative/single-digit ROCE and **Rico Auto** reported a trailing net loss?
1. **In-House SPM & Tooling Moat:** In high-pressure aluminum die-casting (HPDC), **die life is only 80,000–1,20,000 shots**, and precision CNC post-machining determines rejection rates. Craftsman manufactures its own **dies, moulds, and Special Purpose Machines (SPMs) in-house at Coimbatore**, slashing capital cost per line by 25%–30% and keeping scrap rates below 2%.
2. **Distressed Buyout Consolidation (DR Axion + Sunbeam):** Rather than building slow greenfield plants and waiting 4 years for OEM qualification, Craftsman bought **DR Axion India (76% stake in 2023 for ₹375 Cr)**—instantly gaining Hyundai and Kia India as captive cylinder-head/EV-housing clients at >18% EBITDA margins—and in 2024 acquired **Sunbeam Lightweighting Solutions (India's largest 2W die-caster, formerly Amtek group/KKR)** at a distressed slump-sale valuation, closing its high-cost Gurugram plant and shifting production to Bhiwadi/South India.
3. **Sundaram-Clayton's US Trap:** Conversely, Sundaram-Clayton built a capital-heavy greenfield foundry in **Dorchester County, South Carolina (USA)** right into US labor shortages, wage inflation, and power/yield bottlenecks, causing overseas losses to wipe out its profitable Indian operations.

### 🔍 Case Study 2: Why Onshore US Foundries Bleed Cash — Sundaram-Clayton & Bharat Forge US vs. Craftsman Automation
**Indian Protagonists:** *Sundaram-Clayton (South Carolina Foundry Bleed), Bharat Forge (North Carolina Aluminum Plant), Craftsman Automation (Coimbatore/India Consolidation)*  
**US Counterparts:** *Nemak (US/Mexico Aluminum Castings), Ryobi Die Casting USA, Cummins Inc.*

#### 1. The Brutal Lesson of Building Greenfield Metal Plants Inside the United States
Between 2018 and 2023, several Indian auto ancillaries—most notably **Sundaram-Clayton (TVS Group)** in Dorchester County, South Carolina and **Bharat Forge** in North Carolina—decided to build capital-intensive greenfield aluminum die-casting/forging plants *inside the United States* to be "close to US OEMs (Cummins, Daimler, Detroit Three)." **Both suffered multi-year operating losses that dragged down their consolidated balance sheets:**
* **Why US Foundries Destroyed ROCE:**
  1. **Skilled US Foundry Labor Scarcity & Wage Inflation:** Running a high-pressure aluminum die-casting (HPDC) or forging line in South/North Carolina requires skilled tool-and-die technicians and furnace operators in a hot factory environment. Post-COVID US manufacturing wages jumped 35%–45% ($28–$38/hour) with >40% annual worker turnover, causing high scrap rates and broken press downtime (Bharat Forge’s US aluminum unit lost ₹4 Cr EBITDA on ₹461 Cr revenue when a press broke, while Sundaram-Clayton’s consolidated ROCE plunged into negative territory!).
  2. **The Craftsman Automation Counter-Strategy:** In stark contrast, **Craftsman Automation** refused to build greenfield factories in high-cost Western jurisdictions. Instead, Craftsman kept 100% of its manufacturing footprint in India, built its own CNC Special Purpose Machines (SPMs) and dies in Coimbatore, and consolidated the domestic aluminum casting industry by acquiring **DR Axion India** (Hyundai/Kia’s captive supplier) and distressed **Sunbeam Lightweighting**—preserving an **18.6%–21.0% consolidated ROCE (>50% in core powertrain machining)**.

### 🔍 Case Study 3: Railway & Commercial Vehicle Safety Monopolies — ZF CV India & Frontier Springs vs. Wabtec & Knorr-Bremse

Once approved on the RDSO vendor list alongside Wabtec/Knorr-Bremse ecosystems, Frontier Springs commands a **53.60% Gross Margin, ~25% EBITDA Margin, and 43.72% ROCE**—dwarfing auto-spring peers!
