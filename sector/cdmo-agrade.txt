
#AetherIndustries: Something Bigger Than Just Capacity Expansion Is Taking Shape
================================================================================

I went through Aether Industries' Q2FY27 conference call and observed several interesting structural developments. The company is gradually transforming from a specialty chemical manufacturer into a strategic R&D and manufacturing partner for global chemical companies.

Here is what caught my attention.

1. CRAMS & CEM are becoming the growth engine

CRAMS and CEM now contribute more than 65% of total sales. Aether is increasingly moving towards developing complex chemicals for global customers and eventually manufacturing them under long-term contracts. This could improve customer stickiness and business visibility.

2. Baker Hughes partnership is getting bigger

Aether has commercialised another set of oilfield chemicals for Baker Hughes. These products were earlier sourced from Singapore and are now being manufactured in India. The combined opportunity from the two product categories is estimated by management at ₹750–800 crore.

More importantly, Aether is expanding its product relationship with an existing global customer without requiring substantial additional infrastructure.

3. Entry into the semiconductor supply chain

Commercial shipments of specialty chemicals to semiconductor-related customers have started. These materials are used in advanced PCBs, copper-clad laminates and high-performance resins.

A dedicated manufacturing block at Magnum is expected to start commercial production in Q4FY27. Management sees ₹300–400 crore revenue potential from its top two projects at maturity.

4. Magnum is attracting global customers

This is particularly interesting. Out of 16 production blocks at Magnum, management has visibility on 14.

12 blocks are earmarked for CEM and two for LSM.

Several global customers are discussing dedicated manufacturing facilities, including customers from oil & gas and advanced material science.

The interesting part is that some customers are also seeking future capacity reservation rights.

5. A new patented agrochemical molecule

Aether has secured a CEM contract with a global agrochemical innovator for a recently launched patented molecule.

The interesting part is the journey: Aether started with R&D, developed the manufacturing process, completed customer qualification and successfully moved the project towards commercial manufacturing.

Management sees the potential to scale volumes towards 600 tonnes annually.

6. R&D is becoming a customer-acquisition engine

Aether is working on more than 65 CRAMS projects and has moved its R&D operations to 24/7.

During the first half, it added 16 customers and completed more than 20 customer and certification audits.

New opportunities are also emerging in automotive, mining and oil & gas.

7. Dow collaboration could open a new technology platform

Aether and Dow are working on advanced silicon manufacturing technology in India.

The immediate objective is to develop a pilot plant. If successful, this could eventually lead to large-scale commercial manufacturing.

This is a long-term opportunity, but an interesting addition to Aether's technology capabilities.

My biggest takeaway:

Aether's transformation is not simply about building more chemical manufacturing plants.

It is about becoming the manufacturing partner that global companies approach when they have complex chemistry problems.

The growth opportunity lies in converting R&D projects into commercial manufacturing contracts, securing long-term customer relationships and gradually improving factory utilisation.

Going forward, I will closely track three things:

1. Conversion of the CRAMS pipeline into CEM contracts.

2. Commercial ramp-up of semiconductor and Baker Hughes businesses.

3. Magnum capacity utilisation and its impact on EBITDA margins and ROCE.

The real test will be whether this expanding customer pipeline translates into stronger cash flows and better returns on capital.
